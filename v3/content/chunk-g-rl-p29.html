Why the KL term can backfire
Adding a naive KL term to real GRPO training caused the run to collapse shortly after step 50 — the model started outputting non-sensical text and random tokens.
Two compounding causes:
1. IT SCALES WITH SEQUENCE LENGTH

2 . I T C A N B E C O M E T H E O N LY G R A D I E N T

The KL term is computed on summed log-probabilities, so longer responses
automatically produce a larger-magnitude KL term — incentivizing everlonger outputs and destabilizing training.

Once rewards collapse to all-zero, advantages are all zero and the policy
gradient loss vanishes entirely — leaving the KL term as the sole remaining
signal, which then pushes the model toward near-uniform, effectively
random output.
