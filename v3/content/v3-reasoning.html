Motivation

Show what additional inference computation can change with fixed weights, and expose the distinct selection assumptions behind voting, verification and revision.

Prerequisites

  • Sampling, answer extraction and canonicalization.
  • Training gradients versus inference generation.

Notation and units

  • theta remains fixed during inference candidate generation/selection.
  • n: candidate budget; p: per-candidate correctness under the independence toy model.
  • V: deployable verifier, not an evaluation-label lookup.

Assumptions

  • Rational-answer toy voting uses Fraction parsing, not arbitrary code evaluation. Invalid strings are rejected; ties are deterministically broken.
  • The arithmetic verifier checks y²=1764 from the question constraint. It does not look up a gold test answer.

Step-by-step derivation

  1. Training updates theta via an objective gradient. Inference scaling loads fixed theta and spends more computation generating, scoring, voting or revising responses.
  2. Canonicalize equivalent answers before counting;1/2,.5,2/4 should share a vote. Counting full traces instead would split equivalent final answers.
  3. A vote selects the modal extracted answer and can amplify a shared error. A verifier selects a candidate satisfying task constraints, and can abstain if none pass.
  4. Under independent per-sample correctness p, all n failures have probability(1-p)^n; the complement is candidate availability1-(1-p)^n. This is not selector accuracy.
  5. Self-refinement generates feedback and revises under a budget/stopping rule. Feedback can be wrong; retain a checked best-so-far answer and distinguish revision from a weight update.
  6. RL training can teach better generation/selection behavior, but sampling more outputs from fixed weights remains an inference intervention.

What changes at training time versus inference time?

Training changes θ using a gradient and a training objective. Inference-time scaling keeps θ fixed but allocates more computation to generation, selection, verification, or refinement. The two can be combined: a model trained for reasoning can still sample several candidates. More inference computation is not itself evidence that the model weights learned something new.

\text{training: }\theta_{k+1}=\theta_k-\eta\nabla L(\theta_k),\qquad\text{inference: }y^*=\operatorname{Select}(y^{(1)},\ldots,y^{(n)};V),\ \theta\text{ fixed}

Derived toy example: samples [41,41,42] give a majority of 41 even when a correct verifier accepts 42. Voting and verification therefore cannot be treated as equivalent. For independent samples with correctness probability p, the chance at least one of n is correct is 1−(1−p)^n; p=.4,n=4 gives .8704. That is an availability probability under independence, not the accuracy of a selector. Correlated generations and verifier mistakes reduce the benefit.

Normalize answers before voting: whitespace, equivalent fractions, units, and symbolic forms can otherwise split identical answers. A verifier must not accidentally use evaluation targets as a deployable inference oracle. Rule-based exact checks are valuable when available; a learned judge adds its own errors. Self-refinement needs a declared budget, stopping criterion, and retained best-so-far candidate to avoid endless revision.
