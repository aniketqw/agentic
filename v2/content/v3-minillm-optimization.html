Continuation · checked fixture K02 — Reverse KL gradient includes student sampling

Checked continuation mechanism and declared fixture; this is not certification of every inherited claim.

When the student supplies the expectation distribution, its change affects both weights and log probabilities.

Prerequisites: Entropy, cross-entropy, KL, and support, Softmax Jacobian and the CE logit gradient

Notation and assumptions

Exact finite categorical support, q=softmax(z), fixed positive teacher p. L=Σq ln(q/p). This is a one-step derivation, not a full MiniLLM rollout implementation.

Derivation and mechanism

  1. Differentiate L with respect to each q_i to obtain ln(q_i/p_i)+1.
  2. Multiply by the softmax Jacobian.
  3. The constant1 cancels; subtract the q-weighted mean log ratio.
\partial L/\partial z_i=q_i[\log(q_i/p_i)-D_{KL}(q\Vert p)]

Worked example

For student logits[.4,-.1,.8] and teacher[.7,.2,.1], the checker perturbs each logit by±10^-5 and compares central differences. Gradient components sum0 because a common logit shift does not change q.

Limitations

  • Detaching every student probability turns this into a different gradient.
  • Sequence rewards, future returns and stop-gradient choices need their own checks.

Connections to later concepts

  • K03 changes the rollout proposal; future-token returns in a full sequence estimator require additional gradient accounting.

Continuation · checked fixture K03 — Teacher-mixed proposals and importance support

Checked continuation mechanism and declared fixture; this is not certification of every inherited claim.

Changing the sampling distribution requires weights if the original expectation is to be preserved.

Prerequisites: Expectation and population variance, Reverse KL gradient includes student sampling

Notation and assumptions

Target q, proposal m, weight q/m. This exact finite-sum fixture is not self-normalized importance sampling or the full sequence estimator.

Derivation and mechanism

  1. Construct a proposal with support wherever q is positive.
  2. Draw under m and multiply the integrand by q/m.
  3. Verify Σm(q/m)f=Σqf; for sequences specify prefix-product versus token weights.
\mathbb E_m[(q/m)f]=\mathbb E_q[f]

Worked example

q=[.8,.2],m=[.5,.5],f=[2,-1]. Weighted sum=.5×1.6×2+.5×.4×(-1)=1.4, matching target expectation. Proposal[1,0] lacks support and is rejected.

Limitations

  • Clipping weights or self-normalizing generally changes bias.
  • Record teacher-mix coefficient and which policy is detached; token/sequence variants differ.

Derive the gradient before choosing an estimator

For a fixed prompt, let L(θ)=Σ_yqθ(y)log(qθ(y)/p(y)). Assume the teacher is fixed, the distributions are normalized, positive on the student support, and differentiation can pass through the expectation. These assumptions make the following a direct mathematical derivation, not a production trainer.

\nabla L=\sum_y\nabla q_\theta(y)\left[\log\frac{q_\theta(y)}{p(y)}+1\right]=\mathbb E_{q_\theta}\!\left[\left(\log\frac{q_\theta(y)}{p(y)}+1\right)\nabla\log q_\theta(y)\right]

The identity ∇q=q∇log q changes an inaccessible derivative through sampling into a weighted score-function expectation. Since E_q[∇log q]=∇Σ_yq(y)=0, a constant baseline can be subtracted without changing the expected gradient. Individual finite samples still have variance. The reward itself depends on q; blindly treating an arbitrary parameter-dependent reward as fixed is not generally valid. The +1 above comes from differentiating log q and vanishes here only because of normalization.

r_t=\log\frac{p(y_t\mid x,y_{<t})}{q_\theta(y_t\mid x,y_{<t})},\quad R_t=\sum_{u=t}^T r_u,\quad \nabla L=-\mathbb E_q\sum_{t=1}^T R_t\nabla\log q_\theta(y_t\mid x,y_{<t})

Past rewards do not depend on the current sampled action and their expected contribution with its score function is zero; this gives the reward-to-go form. Minimizing L therefore increases student actions when their teacher-relative future reward is favorable. A negative update sign belongs to loss minimization; confusing it with reward ascent reverses the learning signal. The sampled r_t/R_t is detached in a score-function surrogate after deriving the correct estimator, rather than being detached as an unexplained shortcut.

A finite-vocabulary gradient you can check exactly

q=\operatorname{softmax}(z),\quad a_j=\log(q_j/p_j),\quad \frac{\partial L}{\partial z_j}=q_j\left(a_j-\sum_iq_i a_i\right)

Derive this by combining ∂q_i/∂z_j=q_i(δ_ij−q_j) with ∂L/∂q_i=log(q_i/p_i)+1. The constant terms cancel. For the slide 16 distributions the gradient is approximately [−.320412,.091436,.228976]. Gradient descent raises the first logit and lowers the other two. The executable companion compares this gradient against central finite differences and verifies an actual decrease in the objective. This is a one-step categorical check; it does not reproduce a full sequence trainer.

Under a mixture proposal, an exact full-rollout expectation can use the likelihood ratio ∏ₜqₜ(yₜ)/p̃ₜ(yₜ). Products can have high variance. The versioned MiniLLM paper uses a per-step approximation for its prefix importance factors, so its practical update should not be called an exact unbiased sequence estimator. Its training procedure also combines initialization on labeled data, policy refinement, and a language-modeling term. These paper-specific details supplement the qualitative slides; they are not implemented by the toy code here. MiniLLM v4, §2.2–2.3, equations 4–5 and Algorithm 1.

Our explicit future-only convention averages r_(t+1)…r_T by their actual count T−t, and assigns zero to an empty future. For rewards [−.2,−.6,−.4], these averages are [−.5,−.4,0]. This makes indexing and the endpoint testable; it is not a claim of a literal reproduction of the paper’s displayed length-normalization equation.

A complete trainer additionally needs response masks, EOS/padding handling, a declared sampling proposal, stale-policy ratios if reusing rollouts, clipping conventions, teacher/student tokenizer compatibility, and loss normalization. The supplied RL and R1 lessons develop several of these ingredients. Reuse that foundation rather than labeling a short categorical example “MiniLLM training.”
